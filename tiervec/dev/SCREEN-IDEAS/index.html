    Screen ideas¶

    Experimental. The API is not stable and nothing here is wired into a database engine yet. What may change.

    Every idea for the index is listed here before its first run, with its source, what it is expected to do, and the bar it has to pass. The result is added after the run. An idea that fails stays in the list with its numbers, so that it is not tried twice.

    The ideas are tested in two stages (plan on #159): many quick rounds on the screen, at small sizes, and then a confirmation at larger sizes only for the ideas that pass, under rules written before that run. A screen has no timing, and its sizes and data are small and generated, so no default changes on a screen result alone.

    The data of the screen¶

    The first run used 128 dimensions with noise in all of them and queries near stored rows. It ran the exact and tier arms only, and their lowest recall was 0.999 or more at every size, so a difference between two designs had no room to show.

    Harder data is chosen once, by this rule, written before any run on it. Only the baseline arms run while it is chosen (exact, tier, and graph-a10, the live graph at alpha 1.0), never an idea.

    • The candidates are 768 dimensions with --latent 32, 64, 128, 256, and 768, in this order (the two kinds of data).
    • The choice is the first candidate at which, on a10k with seed 1 and the write mix, recallLowest of both tier and graph-a10 is at most 0.97. If none is, the choice is 768.
    • The chosen data, its difficulty (the DATA lines), and the runs of every candidate that was tried are recorded here.

    Step 2: clusters, written before any run of it¶

    • At 768 dimensions with --latent 768, the clusters of a collection (--clusters, 16 on a10k so far) take 8, 4, and 2, in this order: with fewer clusters, more rows are near each query. 2 is the least that lets the distribution move.
    • Only baselines run: exact, tier, graph-a10, and tier-m1 (tier maxSegments=1, where every seal rebuilds all rows into one graph segment: the arm that idea S2 will be compared with).
    • The choice is the first candidate at which, on a10k with seed 1 and the write mix, recallLowest of both graph-a10 and tier-m1 is at most 0.97. If none is, the choice is 2. The default tier is reported and does not decide: it searches several segments with a full beam each, and in step 1 at --latent 768 it computed 6.3 times the distances per search of graph-a10.

    The data of the screen from now on: 768 dimensions, --latent 768, --clusters 8, the first candidate of step 2 that met the threshold. On a10k with seed 1 its queries have an intrinsic dimension of 103.4 and a relative contrast of 1.599. It is harder than real text embeddings are likely to be, so it stretches the designs and says little about the recall a host would see. The default tier stays close to 1 on it.     The lowest recall of graph-a10 and tier-m1 is at the first checkpoint, right after the load, which puts all rows into half of the clusters. A bar on recallLowest would so judge the load and not the churn. results.tsv has a new column, recallChurnMean, the mean recall of the ten checkpoints after the load, and the bars below use it.

    When segments lose on a shape¶

    This is the rule for the comparison with one graph that is updated in place (#160). It was written on 2026-11-28, before any run of a JVector or Lucene arm. The numbers of the live graph on a10k were already known then (S1).

    The tier here is the variant tier, its default settings. The challengers are graph-a10 and graph-a12 (the live graph), jvector and jvector-a10, and the two Lucene variants once their arm exists, lucene and lucene-int8. Tier and challengers run in the screen preset on its data above, in the same run and on one commit, with each of the seeds 1, 2, and 3.

    • The rule judges a shape only if tier built graphs on it (rowsBuiltPerRowWritten above 0). Where it built none, the exact tail answers, and there are no segments to judge.
    • Segments lose to a challenger on a shape if, with each of the three seeds, the challenger has 0 violations that fail a run and no search with fewer than k ids (tooFewHits, which does not fail the run of the JVector arm; this condition was added on 2026-11-28, after a CI smoke on other data showed such searches, and before any run of the arm on the data of the screen), a recallChurnMean that is at most 0.005 below that of tier, a distancesPerSearch that is at most that of tier, and a distancesPerRowWritten that is below that of tier.
    • Segments lose on a shape if they lose to at least one challenger there. Every other outcome is reported as its numbers: a challenger can be better in one column and worse in another, and this rule does not rank that.
    • A distance computation is not a time. A computation on quantized codes counts as one, each library runs its own kernels, and the arms run at fixed settings, not at matched recall. The write cost of Lucene includes the refresh that the replay asks for before every search, which on a shape that probes every write is one refresh per write.
    • A shape that segments lose goes to a timed run, under rules written before that run. A screen changes no default.

    How to add an idea¶

    1. Add a row below with its source, hypothesis, and pass bar, and the status not run. The bar names a shape, a column of results.tsv, and a number.
    2. Add its variants to src/bench/agent-memory-screen.variants, or give them to the run. A change in the library is tried on its branch, as a new setting or a new arm.
    3. Commit, then run the screen on that commit: gh workflow run screen.yml --ref <branch>.
    4. Write the result and the link to the run into the row, and set the status to passed or failed. Change a bar only before the first run.

    The long churn¶

    --turnover 15 --checkpoints 15 on a10k: 150 rounds, each retiring a tenth of the rows, with a checkpoint every 10 rounds, on the data and the write mix of the screen. The rows that arrive come from one cluster after another, so the distribution goes around the 8 clusters many times. The control of the live graph is the arm rebuild, a new graph from the live rows at every checkpoint; the control of the insert merge is tier-m1, whose every merge rebuilds the same layout. The variants are graph-a10, graph-a12, rebuild, tier-m1, and tier-m1-insert. The bars of S4, S5, and S6 were written on 2026-11-28, before any run of them. The bar of S9 was written on 2026-11-28, after the runs of S4 and S6 and before any long churn with two passes.

    The bars of S1 and S2 were changed on 2026-11-28, before their first run on a10k. They named recallLowest (see above), one seed, and for S2 the default tier. At the default maxSegments of 8 the count rule merges the smallest segments, so by the code the largest one, which MergeMode.INSERT needs, is not among them under churn. S2 is so judged with maxSegments 1, where every merge takes the largest segment; tier and tier-insert still run, to show what the default layout does.

    The filter screen¶

    The screen of S7 and S8. It replays no runbook: the program FilterScreen (in src/bench/java) builds one graph and searches it with filters. gh workflow run screen.yml --ref <branch> -f program=filtered-walk runs it once for each of the seeds 1, 2, and 3. The cells, the arms, and the bars were written on 2026-11-28, before the program or any run of it existed.

    Data and graph. 100,000 rows of 768 dimensions, cosine, drawn as the screen draws them (8 clusters, noise of length 0.8 in every dimension), except that each row takes one of all 8 clusters at random (the load of a runbook fills half of them). A query is a new row of a random cluster. No row is deleted. One GraphIndex with the default parameters (M 16, efConstruction 200, alpha 1.0, fp32) is built on one thread and sealed, so it is a SealedGraph, what a tier searches as a segment. All arms search that one graph with k 10 and ef 100, and the same 500 queries are used in every cell.

    Cells. The allowed shares 0.5, 0.1, 0.01, and 0.001 (50,000, 10,000, 1,000, and 100 rows), each with three filters:

    • random: a new uniform choice of rows for each query.
    • positive: the clusters in the order of the similarity of their centre to the query, the most similar first. Whole clusters are allowed in this order until the next one does not fit, and from that one a uniform choice fills the share.
    • negative: the same with the least similar cluster first.

    Each cell reports its correlation as NaviX measures it, here with 100 neighbors: the share of the 100 nearest of all rows that are allowed, divided by the allowed share. 1 means no correlation.

    Arms.

    • walk: the shipped filtered search with filterScanBelow 0. The walk traverses every neighbor and collects those that pass. When it has computed more than max(50 * ef, rows / 20) distances, 5,000 here, and has collected fewer than ef, an exact scan follows.
    • scanfirst: the shipped filtered search with filterScanBelow 1: the upper layers, the 256-row sample of the share, then the exact scan.
    • acorn (S7) and navix (S8): benchmark code only. The upper layers are searched as shipped, without the filter. On layer 0 the node reached there is a candidate whether it passes or not, and apart from it only rows that pass become candidates. A walk stops when its nearest candidate is farther than the worst of ef collected rows, or when it has computed more than 5,000 distances on layer 0. A walk that ends with fewer than ef rows collected, for either reason or because no candidate is left, is followed by the exact scan. The predicate is asked at most once per row in a walk; a scan asks it again.

    acorn, from the Lucene source above, with r the share of the rows of the 256-row sample that pass (Lucene knows the count of its filter) and M 16:

    • At r of 0.6 or more (Lucene's default threshold) the shipped walk is used.
    • The expansion multiplier is round(min(1 / r, M / 2)), and the minimum to find is round(min(max(0, 1 / r - 2 * M), M)).
    • At each candidate, the neighbors not seen before are split into passing and failing ones. The target is the degree of the candidate times min(multiplier, 1 / (1 - f)), rounded down, where f is the number of failing ones over the degree. If f is above 0.10 and fewer passing rows than the target were found, the neighbors of the failing ones are read in list order, until the target is reached or the rows looked at reach 2 * M * multiplier - 1. A failing row of this second step is expanded as well while fewer passing rows than the minimum were found.
    • The passing rows found this way are then scored.

    navix, from section 3 of the paper, with D 32, the degree limit of layer 0 (the paper writes M for the limit that its rule counts to):

    • At each candidate, s is the share of all its layer-0 neighbors that pass.
    • s of 0.5 or more (onehop-s): score the passing neighbors.
    • Otherwise, if s * (D + 1) * D is below 3 * D (blind): score the passing neighbors, then read the neighbor lists of all neighbors in list order and score passing rows from them, until D rows were scored at this candidate.
    • Otherwise (directed): compute the distance of every neighbor, passing or not, score the passing ones, then read the neighbor lists in the order of increasing distance of the neighbor and score passing rows from them, until D rows were scored at this candidate.
    • "Scored at this candidate" counts passing rows that were not scored before in this walk.

    Figures. Per seed, cell, and arm, in filter.tsv:

    • recall: recall@10 against a brute force scan of the allowed rows, made by the benchmark's own loop.
    • distancesPerQuery: distance computations of a query on all layers, a scan included, counted by the instrumented kernels. The brute force of the benchmark is not counted.
    • filterCallsPerQuery: calls of the predicate, with those of the share sample and of a scan.
    • scanRate: the share of the queries answered by an exact scan (1 for scanfirst).
    • shortAnswers: queries with fewer than 10 hits.

    A hit that the filter does not allow fails the run.

    Pass bars, judged for S7 and for S8 separately. Each holds with each of the seeds 1, 2, and 3, with the arm and walk from the same run:

    1. Recall: in each of the 12 cells, recall of the arm is at least that of walk minus 0.01.
    2. Cost: in each of the 12 cells, distancesPerQuery of the arm is at most 1.1 times that of walk.
    3. Gain: in each of the three cells with share 0.1, distancesPerQuery of the arm is at most 0.7 times the smaller of those of walk and scanfirst.

    An idea passes when all three hold. filterCallsPerQuery and scanRate have no bar: what a call costs depends on the predicate of the host, so the timed comparison has to weigh them. A screen times nothing. A pass only sends an arm to the timed comparison of #162, under the adoption rule that is to be written there before it, and no default or threshold changes.

    Why alpha 1.2 failed¶

    Until the change of S4, GraphIndex took the candidates of a list in increasing distance and dropped one only when alpha times its distance to a kept neighbor was at most its distance to the node. It did this in one pass and stopped when the list was full. On this data the candidates of a node are nearly all about as far from each other as from the node, so at alpha 1.2 the rule dropped almost none, the list was full before the far candidates were looked at, and the far candidates are the ones that lead to other clusters. The layer-0 graph then came apart under churn. Low out-degrees were not the cause: they were no more common at alpha 1.2.
